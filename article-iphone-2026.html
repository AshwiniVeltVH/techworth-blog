Is Buying an iPhone Worth It in 2026?

The iPhone is still a premium phone. But does that premium price make sense when affordable Android phones can do most everyday tasks just as easily?

October 2026   •   8 min read

iPhone 16 — one of Apple's current mainstream iPhone models.

Buying a smartphone in 2026 is a little different from a few years ago.

You can spend a large amount of money on a premium phone, but you can also find phones at a much lower price that handle messaging, social media, streaming, photography, gaming and everyday apps without much trouble.

That brings us to an obvious question: Is buying an iPhone still worth it?

The answer depends less on the Apple logo and more on what you actually expect from your phone.

What Does the iPhone 16 Offer?

The iPhone 16 is built around Apple's A18 chip and includes a 48MP Fusion main camera, a 12MP Ultra Wide camera, an Action button and Camera Control.

Apple also focuses heavily on long-term software support, ecosystem integration and the overall experience of using the device.

These things are important because the price of an iPhone is not simply paying for the processor or camera hardware.

The Price Changes the Conversation

Apple currently lists the iPhone 16 in India at around ₹89,900.

That is a significant amount of money for a smartphone.

And this is where the comparison becomes interesting.

At considerably lower prices, Android phones now offer high-refresh-rate AMOLED displays, large batteries, fast charging and capable processors.

So What Are You Actually Paying For?

When you buy an iPhone, you are paying for more than specifications on a box.

  • Apple's hardware and software integration
  • Long-term software support
  • Apple's ecosystem
  • Video and camera processing
  • Build quality and design
  • Resale value considerations
  • A consistent overall user experience

Whether these advantages are worth the extra money is something each buyer has to decide for themselves.

When Does an iPhone Make Sense?

An iPhone can make sense if you specifically value Apple's ecosystem and want to keep your phone for several years.

It can also be attractive if you already use products such as a Mac, iPad or Apple Watch and want everything to work together.

Someone who prioritizes video recording, consistent performance and long-term software support may also place more value on an iPhone than someone who mainly uses WhatsApp, Instagram, YouTube and basic apps.

When Should You Think Twice?

The biggest reason to think twice is simple: the price.

If buying an iPhone would put significant pressure on your monthly budget, it is worth comparing alternatives before making the purchase.

A smartphone should make your life easier, not turn every monthly expense into a calculation.

What Can You Get for Much Less?

The Android market has become extremely competitive.

For example, the OnePlus Nord 5 is listed in India from around ₹33,999.

It offers features such as a high-refresh-rate AMOLED display, a large battery, fast charging and a powerful Snapdragon processor.

Samsung's Galaxy A27 5G is another example. It starts around ₹28,999 and focuses on features such as a 120Hz Super AMOLED display, a 5000mAh battery and long software-support commitments.

More Megapixels Don't Automatically Mean Better Cameras

Smartphone camera comparisons can become confusing because manufacturers often advertise large megapixel numbers.

But camera quality also depends on the sensor, image processing, stabilization, lenses and software.

So a 50MP camera should not automatically be considered better than a 48MP camera.

Battery and Charging

This is another area where Android phones often offer impressive specifications for less money.

Large batteries and fast charging are common in phones costing considerably less than an iPhone.

However, battery capacity alone does not tell you how long a phone will actually last. Software optimisation, processor efficiency and usage patterns also matter.

What About Software Updates?

Software support is one of the areas where long-term ownership becomes important.

Apple has historically supported iPhones for many years, while Android manufacturers have also improved their update policies significantly.

That means buyers should look at the specific phone's official software-support policy rather than assuming every Android phone has short support.

What If Your Monthly Income Is Around ₹25,000–₹30,000?

Imagine someone earning around ₹25,000–₹30,000 per month.

Buying an ₹89,900 phone would represent several months of income before considering rent, food, travel, savings and other expenses.

That does not automatically mean they should not buy one. Personal priorities are different.

But it does mean the buyer should compare the opportunity cost.

Think beyond the phone.

Before spending heavily on a smartphone, consider emergency savings, monthly expenses, education, investments and other financial goals.

When Does Android Make More Sense?

Android can make more sense when your priority is getting a strong collection of hardware features without spending close to ₹90,000.

For many people, a phone around ₹25,000–₹40,000 can already handle their daily requirements comfortably.

If your main activities are messaging, social media, YouTube, photography, navigation and normal productivity, you may not need an expensive flagship.

When Can the iPhone Still Be Worth It?

The iPhone can still be a reasonable purchase if you specifically want Apple's ecosystem, camera and video experience, software environment or long-term ownership.

If those features genuinely matter to you, comparing only processor speed or battery capacity will not tell the complete story.

iPhone 16: Things to Consider

Potential Advantages

  • Strong performance
  • Apple ecosystem
  • Long-term software support
  • Strong video capabilities
  • Consistent overall experience

Things to Consider

  • High upfront price
  • Accessories can add to the cost
  • Android phones offer strong hardware at lower prices
  • Not everyone needs flagship-level performance

So, Is Buying an iPhone Worth It in 2026?

There is no single answer that applies to everyone.

If you want Apple's ecosystem, long-term software support, strong video capabilities and the overall iPhone experience, the price may make sense for you.

If your main goal is simply to get a fast and reliable smartphone for everyday use, there are considerably cheaper options worth considering.

The most important question is not: “Can I afford an iPhone?”

It is: “What do I actually need my phone to do?”

Once you answer that honestly, choosing a phone becomes much easier.
