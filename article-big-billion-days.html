Big Billion Days 2026: How to Get the Best Smartphone Deals

A lower price does not automatically mean a good deal. Here's how to check the real cost of a smartphone before clicking Buy.

October 2026   •   7 min read

Big Sale ≠ Automatic Bargain

Learn how to calculate the actual price before buying a smartphone.

Major sales can reduce smartphone prices, but the final price depends on discounts, offers and eligibility.

Big smartphone sales can be exciting.

You see a phone that normally costs a lot of money suddenly showing a large discount, and the first thought is often: “I should buy this now.”

But this is exactly when it is worth slowing down.

A sale price can look impressive while the actual saving is smaller than expected once bank conditions, exchange value and other requirements are considered.

So instead of asking only whether a phone is on sale, ask: “What will I actually pay?”

First, Know the Normal Price

Before a major sale begins, find out the phone's normal selling price.

Don't rely only on the crossed-out MRP.

A phone may have an MRP of ₹50,000 but regularly sell for ₹38,000. If the sale price becomes ₹35,000, the meaningful comparison is with the regular ₹38,000 price, not simply the ₹50,000 MRP.

Compare sale price vs normal selling price.

MRP is not always the most useful number for deciding whether you are actually getting a good deal.

Check the Bank Offer Carefully

Bank discounts can make a smartphone deal look much better.

But the advertised discount may have conditions.

For example, an offer may require:

  • A particular bank card
  • A minimum transaction value
  • A specific payment method
  • A maximum discount limit
  • Eligibility for a particular customer or card

Always read the actual offer terms before assuming the entire advertised discount will apply to you.

Be Careful With Exchange Offers

Exchange discounts can sometimes reduce the effective purchase price significantly.

But the advertised maximum exchange value is not necessarily the amount you will receive for your phone.

The final value can depend on:

  • Your old phone model
  • Storage capacity
  • Physical condition
  • Screen condition
  • Functional condition
  • Inspection results

If the deal only looks attractive because of an unusually large exchange value, calculate the price again without assuming you will receive the maximum amount.

What About iPhone Deals?

iPhones are often watched closely during major shopping events because their normal prices are relatively high.

A sale can therefore make an older or current-generation iPhone more interesting to buyers who were already planning to purchase one.

But don't let a discount percentage make the decision for you.

For example, if an iPhone drops from a higher price to a lower sale price, ask whether the resulting price fits your budget and whether you actually need the features.

A discount does not turn an expensive phone into an affordable phone automatically.

Should You Consider an Older iPhone During a Sale?

This can be worth considering depending on the price.

When a newer model is available, an older generation can sometimes become more attractive if the discount is large enough.

But check the exact model, storage capacity, warranty, seller and software-support situation before buying.

Don't buy an older model simply because the discount percentage looks large.

Calculate the Real Price

This is the most useful habit you can develop during major shopping sales.

Product price − eligible bank discount − eligible coupon − eligible exchange benefit =
Actual price you pay

The word eligible is important.

If you don't have the required bank card or your old phone doesn't qualify for the advertised exchange value, you cannot count that discount as guaranteed savings.

Don't Buy Something Just Because It Is Cheaper

This sounds obvious, but major sales are designed around urgency.

Limited-time banners, countdowns and large discount labels can make a purchase feel more urgent than it really is.

Before buying, ask:

  • Was I already planning to replace my phone?
  • Does this phone solve my actual needs?
  • Is the storage enough?
  • Will I keep it for several years?
  • Is the final price comfortable for my budget?

Make Your Shortlist Before the Sale

One of the easiest ways to avoid impulse purchases is to decide what you want before the sale starts.

Example shortlist

  1. Choose your maximum budget.
  2. Select three or four phones.
  3. Record their normal prices.
  4. Check storage variants.
  5. Note important features.
  6. Wait for the sale.
  7. Compare the final prices.

This makes it much easier to recognise a genuinely useful discount.

Check the Exact Model and Storage

Smartphone prices can vary significantly depending on RAM and storage configuration.

A sale headline may advertise the lowest configuration, while the version you actually want costs considerably more.

Always check the exact:

  • Model
  • RAM
  • Storage
  • Colour
  • Network variant

Check the Seller

Price isn't the only thing that matters when buying an expensive electronic product online.

Check who is selling the product and review the platform's return, replacement and warranty information.

This becomes particularly important when the price difference between sellers is unusually large.

Don't Forget Warranty and Returns

Before placing an order, check:

  • Warranty duration
  • Who provides the warranty
  • Return eligibility
  • Replacement conditions
  • Whether the product is new and sealed

Keep your invoice and order details after purchase.

Should You Buy Immediately When the Sale Starts?

Not necessarily.

Prices and offers can change during a large shopping event.

However, waiting also carries a risk that a particular variant may go out of stock.

The practical approach is to decide your acceptable price beforehand.

If the phone reaches a price you are comfortable with and the conditions are suitable, you can make the purchase without trying to predict every later price movement.

A Simple Example

Imagine a phone has a regular selling price of ₹40,000.

During a sale:

  • Sale price: ₹36,000
  • Eligible bank discount: ₹2,000
  • Eligible coupon: ₹500
  • Exchange benefit: ₹3,000

If you genuinely qualify for all those offers, the effective price would be:

₹36,000 − ₹2,000 − ₹500 − ₹3,000

= ₹30,500

But if you don't have the required bank card or your exchange device doesn't qualify for the advertised amount, the actual price will be higher.

Big Billion Days Smartphone Checklist

  1. Know the phone's normal selling price.
  2. Compare the exact storage variant.
  3. Check the seller.
  4. Read bank-offer conditions.
  5. Don't assume maximum exchange value.
  6. Check warranty and return rules.
  7. Calculate the final price.
  8. Compare with your pre-sale shortlist.
  9. Buy only if the phone actually fits your needs.

Set a Personal Maximum Price

Before opening the sale page, decide the highest amount you are comfortable spending.

For example, if your maximum is ₹35,000, don't let a ₹39,999 phone convince you that you are only ₹4,999 away from a better deal.

Your budget should remain your budget.

Final Thoughts

Big Billion Days and other major sales can be useful opportunities to buy technology at a lower effective price.

But the biggest discount number on the screen isn't always the most important number.

Check the normal price, bank eligibility, exchange value, exact model, seller and warranty.

Most importantly, decide what you actually need before the sale begins.

A smartphone is a good deal when the final price, features and your needs all make sense together.
